# Emberbound · Paper-cut Lightbox · 45 s · vertical 1080×1920 (YouTube Shorts) · 30 fps

An original winter legend: a girl from a mountain village saves a fallen dragon by giving it her lantern's flame, and from then on their fates are bound. English narration (Kokoro, `bf_emma`), burned-in subtitles, original score. Nothing is taken from any existing film: new characters, new story, no names.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **One window, one year.** A locked lamp: the girl's lit window, the dragon seen only as a shadow passing over it across four seasons. Quiet and elegant, but a Short needs its event to happen on screen in the first seconds, and "the dragon" would never be seen properly.
2. **A relay of light.** The flame passes from moon → lantern → dragon → village, each a small box with its own colour ladder. Clever, but five places in 45 s on a phone screen is too many to read.
3. **A vertical stack, top to bottom and back up.** The vertical frame *is* the lightbox's stack: we start in darkness with one pinhole (the moon), follow a falling "star" down through sky, peaks, firs and village to the snow, where it turns out to be a dragon; the lamp goes dark as its heart fails; the girl's lantern relights it, the whole box lights up layer by layer, and we follow the dragon back up to the moon, then pull out to see the whole lamp — the moon, the dragon and the girl's tiny lantern in one box.

**Chosen: 3.** A 9:16 frame is naturally a tilt down and up the stack; the fall gives a hook in the first 3 seconds, and the ending pull-out shows both fates (dragon and lantern) sharing one light — the story's point in one image.

## 2. Logline and arc

On the coldest night of winter, a girl gives a dying dragon the only light she has — and from then on, whenever it flies, her lantern burns.

- **Setup** (0–10 s): darkness → pinhole → moon; a "star" falls; tilt down the whole stack; it crashes in the snow.
- **Turn** (10–23 s): it is a dragon; its heart is going out; the girl walks out with her lantern; the lamp goes dark (silence).
- **Climax** (23–30 s): she opens her lantern; a thread of light flows into the dragon's heart; the heart flares on "light"; the whole box lights up layer by layer.
- **Ending** (30–45 s): the dragon rises; we tilt up after it, then pull out of the box: the dragon crossing the moon, her lantern glowing far below; the title cuts itself in paper; rack focus into warm light.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Commercial paper-cut shadow-box lamps** (the layer stack, the rim light, light burning through holes) and **Lotte Reiniger-style silhouette animation** (jointed figures that move by rotation only).
- **Learn:** the value ladder as depth; light as the main event; jointed silhouettes that read at thumbnail size; patience — one action per shot.
- **Don't take:** any lamp design, any frame or character from any silhouette film, anything from the reference video the user pointed to (only its genre: a girl, a dragon, a shared fate).

## 6. Cue map

- 72 BPM, 4/4: beat 0.833 s, bar 3.333 s. Hits on the grid: crash 10.0 (bar 3), lamp off 20.0 (bar 6), lamp back 23.33 (bar 7), heart flare 25.0 (bar 7 beat 3), rise 30.0 (bar 9), moon crossing 40.0 (bar 12).
- **Night** (0–10): harp ostinato in A minor, high violin harmonic pad, hand chimes on the pinhole and the windows.
- **Snow** (10–20): soft cellos + violas, flute melody (bamboo-flute colour), harp thinning; single frame-drum stroke on the crash.
- **Dark** (20–23.33): silence.
- **Light** (23.33–30): harp returns, strings swell into A major (the flare is a C → C# shift on 25.0), chimes cascade with the stagger.
- **Flight** (30–45): full strings + flute theme, harp arpeggios; gong (soft) on the moon crossing; final A-major chord ringing out under the rack focus.
